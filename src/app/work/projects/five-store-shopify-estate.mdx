## The setup

Five live Shopify storefronts, one per market:

| Store | Market | Currency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [ringsofaustralia.com.au](https://ringsofaustralia.com.au) | Australia | AUD |
| [ringsofuk.com](https://ringsofuk.com) | United Kingdom | GBP |
| [ringsofgermany.com](https://ringsofgermany.com) | Germany | EUR |
| [preciouscarbon.com](https://www.preciouscarbon.com) | United States | USD |
| [premexa.in](https://premexa.in) | India | INR |

Each has its own custom Liquid theme, its own catalogue, its own pricing, tax and shipping configuration, and its own merchandising.

## The architectural decision

The obvious move is one store with multi-market routing. We deliberately did not do that, and the reasoning is the part worth explaining.

Each market has **its own legal entity, its own inventory and its own merchandising team**. Germany buys differently from Australia; the UK entity holds stock in Hatton Garden that the US entity cannot sell. A single store with market-level overrides would have forced one product catalogue and one merchandising owner onto five businesses that genuinely are not one business.

So: separate stores.

The honest trade-off is **duplicated theme deploys**. Five stores means five places for a bug fix to land, and drift between them is the failure mode that eats teams alive.

## How the trade-off is paid for

A **shared section library**. The themes are per-brand, but the sections, blocks and JSON templates underneath them are a common system built with the Shopify CLI — theme app extensions where the behaviour is genuinely shared, per-store overrides where it is not.

The same applies below the theme: one analytics and tagging layer deployed identically across the estate, so reporting is comparable market to market instead of five dialects of the same event.

If you view-source two of these stores, you will find the same asset bootstrap and the same tag container. That is the section library showing through.

## What this involves day to day

- Theme development with the **Shopify CLI** — sections, blocks, JSON templates, theme app extensions
- Per-store catalogue, pricing, tax and shipping configuration
- Currency and locale handling per storefront, including German-language content
- Keeping five themes from drifting while still letting each brand look like itself
- Core Web Vitals across all five, on mobile, in markets with very different network conditions
