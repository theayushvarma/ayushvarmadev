## The problem

Every jewellery manufacturer already has 3D models. They sit in CAD files — `.3dm` from Rhino, plus the usual `.stl`, `.obj`, `.fbx` exports — used for manufacturing, then thrown away. Meanwhile the same company pays a photographer to shoot the physical piece for the website.

The model is right there. It is just in a format the web cannot use, lit for machining rather than for looking at, and untagged: nothing in the file says *this surface is gold, that one is a diamond.*

## What I built

[i3dStudio](https://i3dstudio.vercel.app) takes the CAD file and returns a viewer a customer can spin, zoom and try on.

**Ingestion.** Accepts 3DM, GLB, GLTF, STL, OBJ, FBX, DAE and PLY. An import analyser inspects geometry and material slots to **auto-detect metals, stones and prongs**, so a raw upload becomes a configurable product without anyone hand-tagging surfaces.

**Rendering.** Built on **Three.js and React Three Fiber**. Diamonds are the hard part — a stone rendered with a normal PBR material looks like grey glass. Getting it right needs real refraction and dispersion, plus a studio lighting rig that reads as jewellery photography. The library ships 115+ PBR materials and 100+ starter designs.

**AR try-on**, in three modes because no single one works everywhere:

1. live camera with hand tracking
2. photo upload, for users who will not grant camera permission
3. a studio model-hand preview, which always works

…with ring-size estimation in US, UK and EU sizing.

**Distribution.** This is the part that makes it a product rather than a demo. The viewer ships as a **one-line, domain-locked iframe embed**, plus a **REST API, a typed SDK and an OpenAPI spec**. A Shopify merchant drops in one tag; a larger integrator drives it programmatically.

**Rendering pipeline.** Offline 4K stills and turntable video export, metered by a credit system across four billing tiers.

## What was hard

Photoreal and real-time pull in opposite directions. Refraction is expensive, and a ring viewer that stutters on a mid-range phone is worse than a photograph. Most of the engineering was budget management: how much optical realism can be bought before frame time collapses, and what can be baked ahead of time instead of solved per frame.

The second hard problem was the messiness of real CAD. Manufacturing files are not built for rendering — inconsistent scale, arbitrary transforms, no material conventions. The analyser exists because assuming clean input meant it broke on nearly every real file.

## Stack

Three.js · React Three Fiber · WebGL · Next.js · TypeScript · Node.js · GLTF/GLB pipelines
