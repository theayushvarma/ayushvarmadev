## The problem

Selling an engagement ring online is not selling a product — it is selling a *combination*. A customer picks a setting, then a diamond, then a metal, then a size, then an engraving. The catalogue is combinatorial, and most of the inventory does not physically exist yet: it is a virtual feed of stones from suppliers that changes hourly.

A standard product page cannot express that. Merchants were either hand-building thousands of variants or losing the sale.

## What I built

An interactive ring configurator that merges **physical and virtual diamond inventory** into a single buying flow, then shipped it as a distributable product on two platforms.

**Shopify App Store** — [Diamond Engagement RingBuilder](https://apps.shopify.com/tdp-ring-builder), live since July 2024, published under The Diamond Port's Partner account. I took it through Shopify's app review.

**WordPress.org** — [TDP Ring Builder](https://wordpress.org/plugins/tdp-ring-builder/), with WooCommerce integration, for merchants who are not on Shopify.

Same product logic, two completely different host platforms and two separate review bars to clear.

## Architecture

The Shopify app is an embedded admin app built with **Remix and Node.js**, rendering React through **App Bridge** and **Polaris** so it looks and behaves like native Shopify admin. OAuth and session storage follow the standard Shopify app pattern; the storefront side talks to **Admin GraphQL** and reacts to **webhooks** for inventory and order events.

The interesting constraint was the feed. Supplier stone inventory is large, changes constantly, and cannot be mirrored into Shopify products without blowing past variant limits. So the configurator treats virtual stones as a live query against the feed and only materialises a concrete line item at the moment of add-to-cart.

Merchants configure the rest without a developer:

- custom markups applied per stone category
- configurable colours, filters and search controls
- engraving options
- automated ordering back to the supplier once a sale completes

## Shipping to two marketplaces

The two platforms disagree about almost everything — asset loading, auth, admin UI conventions, how you are allowed to inject into a cart. The part that transferred cleanly was the domain logic: stone matching, price assembly, size handling. The part that did not was every integration boundary.

That split is the actual lesson. Keeping the configurator's core independent of the host platform is what made the second marketplace a port rather than a rewrite.

## Outcome

Live on the Shopify App Store for two years at $1,499 one-time, rated 5.0. Listed on WordPress.org with active installs. Both listings are public and clickable above — which is the point: this is not a private repo I can only describe.
